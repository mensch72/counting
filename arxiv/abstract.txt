We describe a transfer-matrix method for counting unlabelled graded lattices by their rank layers, and apply it to distributive and to modular lattices. Earlier counts generate the lattices one at a time. Here all lower parts of lattices that look the same from above are merged into one state. For a distributive lattice J(P) the state after rank r is the poset of those elements of P that lie in some but not all down-sets of size r, together with the symmetry group induced on it; for a modular lattice it is the poset of pairwise meets of the elements of rank r. We prove that these states determine all possible continuations up to isomorphism, so that the work grows with the number of states and not with the number of lattices. For modular lattices the number of states is reduced further by a bound that rests on self-duality of the class. We obtain the number of distributive lattices up to 160 elements; the previous record was 60. For modular lattices we reproduce the known numbers up to 38 elements in 142 minutes on one processor core. The frontier for modular lattices also serves for semimodular lattices, for which we obtain two further values (26 and 27 elements). For distributive lattices with layers of bounded size the transfer matrix is a finite automaton; from it we derive the certified bounds 1.7265363 and 1.84337061 for the exponential growth rates of the vertically indecomposable and of all distributive lattices (previously 1.7250 and 1.84), and 2.3951 for vertically indecomposable modular lattices (previously 2.3122). The 60 previously known values for distributive lattices take 0.1 seconds. The new values are supported by the known ones, by duality checks, by brute force over all posets on at most 11 points, and by agreement of the two programs on distributive lattices.
